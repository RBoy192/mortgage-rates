Rates Explained: Mortgages

Getting a mortgage, step by step

From the first chat to getting your keys.

In brief
  • Talk to a bank or a mortgage broker first.
  • Get pre-approved, so you know your budget.
  • Make an offer, then apply for real.
  • A lawyer (or notary in Quebec) handles the closing, and you get your keys.

1. The first chat

It starts with a conversation, with a bank or a mortgage broker. You talk about the home you want, what you earn, what you owe, and how much you've saved. No forms yet.

2. Pre-approval

The lender looks at your money to work out the most it would lend you, and at what rate1. It checks your credit, and asks for papers like these1:

  • ID.
  • Proof of your job and pay, like a recent pay stub.
  • Proof you have the down payment and closing costs.
  • What else you own, and what you owe.
  • If you're self-employed: two years of tax notices from the Canada Revenue Agency.

Employed or self-employed?

Either way, the lender wants to see that your income is real and that it will keep coming. What changes is how you show it.

Proof of the jobYour position with your employer1Proof your business exists and how long it's run, like a business licence, GST/HST returns or business bank statements3
How longHow long you've worked for your employer1About 2 years in business, or in the same line of work, is recommended3
Income usedYour salary or hourly pay1The income on your tax returns, after business expenses. Some lenders may add part of it back3

Why it matters if you work for yourself: business expenses you claim lower your tax, but they also lower the income the lender sees. Less income on paper means a smaller mortgage. If you've run your business for less than 2 years, you can still qualify, but expect more questions about your savings, your contracts and your work history3.

Many lenders will hold that rate for you for 60 to 130 days1. A pre-approval is a "probably yes", not a final yes. The lender can still say no later, for example if the home doesn't meet its rules1.

3. Find a home and make an offer

Your offer usually has conditions: it only goes ahead if your mortgage is approved, and if the home inspection is OK. You get a few days to clear them.

4. Apply for real

Now you send the lender your accepted offer. They check everything again, including the stress test from GDS and TDS.

5. The appraisal

The lender checks that the home is worth what you're paying.

6. Insurance, if you put down less than 20%

The lender sends your file to a mortgage insurer. Insured vs uninsured explains how that works.

7. Approval

You get a commitment letter with the rate and terms. You sign it, then tell the seller your financing condition is cleared.

8. The lawyer (or notary in Quebec)

You give your lawyer the rest of the price (your down payment) plus your closing costs2. They also check the home really belongs to the seller, and you sign the mortgage papers with them. Your lender will usually want proof of home insurance before closing.

9. Closing day

The lender sends the mortgage money to your lawyer. Your lawyer pays the seller, registers the home in your name, and gives you the keys2.

10. After you move in

Your first payment is usually about a month later. Check how much you can pay off early each year without a penalty, in case you get extra money.
