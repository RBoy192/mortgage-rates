Rates Explained: GICs

GIC ladders

Split your money across terms, so a part comes due every year.

In brief
  • Split your money into 5 equal parts.
  • Put them in 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5-year GICs.
  • Every year, one part comes due. Put it in a new 5-year GIC.

Long terms usually pay more, but they lock your money away. Short terms free your money sooner, but usually pay less. A GIC ladder gives you a bit of both1.

How to build one

  1. Split your money into 5 equal parts. For $10,000, that's five parts of $2,000.
  2. Put one part in each term: 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 years1.
  3. Next year, the 1-year GIC comes due. Put it in a new 5-year GIC1.
  4. Keep going. After a few years, all your GICs are 5-year GICs (the higher rates), but one still comes due every year.

Why people like it

  • Some of your money comes free every year, in case you need it1.
  • If rates go up, you get to use the new rates every year. If they go down, most of your money is still at the old rates1.
  • Over time you can earn more than with short terms only1.
