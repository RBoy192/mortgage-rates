Rates Explained: GICs

Is my money safe?

GICs are one of the safest places for your money in Canada.

In brief
  • At most banks, CDIC insures your GICs and deposits.
  • It covers up to $100,000 per category, per bank.
  • Credit unions are covered by their province instead.

Banks rarely fail in Canada. But if one did, deposit insurance protects your money.

At a bank

The Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) is a federal Crown corporation. It insures eligible deposits at its member banks. You don't sign up or pay for it: it's free and automatic1.

  • It covers GICs, other term deposits, and savings and chequing accounts, in Canadian or foreign currency1.
  • It covers up to $100,000, including interest, in each category at each member bank1.
  • Categories include deposits in your name, joint deposits, and deposits in a TFSA, RRSP, RRIF, FHSA, RESP or RDSP1. So you can be covered for more than $100,000 in total.
  • It doesn't cover stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs or crypto1.

CDIC says no one has ever lost a dollar it insured1. You can check whether a bank is a member on CDIC's website1.

At a credit union

Most credit unions are covered by a deposit insurer in their own province, not CDIC. The limits depend on the province. For example, in Ontario, eligible deposits are insured up to $250,000, and deposits in registered accounts like a TFSA or RRSP have no limit2. Ask your credit union what applies to you.
