Term vs amortization

Key points
  • Amortization is the total time to pay off the mortgage, often 25 to 30 years.
  • The term is how long your current rate and conditions last, usually 5 years or less.
  • When a term ends you renew, often at a different rate, and you can shop around.

Amortization is how long it would take to pay the mortgage off completely, commonly 25-30 years1. With less than 20% down, the longest is 25 years, or 30 years for first-time buyers and buyers of new builds. With 20% or more down, your lender sets the maximum. Term is how long your current deal lasts. Terms run from a few months to 5 years or more, and most Canadian mortgage holders have a term of 5 years or less1. When a term ends you renew, and the lender offers a new rate for the next term. That's why one amortization usually means several terms.
