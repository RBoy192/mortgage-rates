Mortgages Explained

Term vs amortization

Every mortgage has two time periods, and they mean different things.

In brief
  • Amortization is how long it takes to pay off the whole mortgage, often 25 to 30 years.
  • The term is how long your current rate and conditions last, usually 5 years or less.
  • When a term ends you renew, and you can compare offers from other lenders.

Amortization

The amortization is the total time it would take to pay off the whole mortgage, commonly 25 to 30 years1. If your down payment is under 20%, the longest allowed is 25 years, or 30 years for first-time buyers and buyers of newly built homes2. With 20% or more down, your lender sets the limit.

Term

The term is how long your current agreement lasts. Your interest rate and conditions stay set for this period. Terms range from a few months to 5 years or more, and most Canadians with a mortgage have a term of 5 years or less1.

Why the difference matters

When a term ends, you renew. Your lender offers a new rate for the next term, and you're free to compare offers from other lenders. Because the term is shorter than the amortization, most people renew several times before their mortgage is paid off.
