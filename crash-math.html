Do the math yourself

Key points
  • Your payment depends on three things: how much you borrow, the rate and the amortization.
  • Fixed rates in Canada are worked out twice a year, which the formula takes into account.
  • You can check any lender's numbers yourself with the formula or the offline calculator.
  1. Mortgage amount = home price − down payment. If you put down less than 20%, add the insurance premium too.
  2. Rate per month (call it i). Fixed: (1 + annual rate ÷ 2)^(1/6) − 1. The Interest Act requires a mortgage to state its rate calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance1, and fixed rates are normally worked out half-yearly (twice a year). Variable: annual rate ÷ 12. This site's calculators assume monthly compounding for variable rates, a common lender practice, so check your lender's mortgage agreement.
  3. Number of payments (n) = years × 12
  4. Monthly payment = mortgage × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^(−n))
  5. Each month: interest = balance × i. Principal = payment − interest. New balance = balance − principal. Then repeat.
  6. Equity = what the home is worth − what you still owe

The whole formula in one line (steps 2 to 4 combined):

Fixed rate: Payment = P × [(1 + r÷2)^(1÷6) − 1] ÷ [1 − (1 + r÷2)^(−2 × y)] Variable rate: Payment = P × (r÷12) ÷ [1 − (1 + r÷12)^(−12 × y)]

P = mortgage amount (plus any insurance premium) · r = yearly rate as a decimal (4.84% is 0.0484) · y = amortization in years
