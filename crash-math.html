Mortgages Explained

Do the math yourself

How to check any mortgage payment yourself.

In brief
  • Your payment depends on three things: how much you borrow, the rate and the amortization.
  • Fixed rates in Canada are calculated twice a year, and the formula allows for that.
  • You can check any lender's numbers with the formula or the offline calculator.

Step by step

  1. Mortgage amount = home price − down payment. With less than 20% down, add the insurance premium.
  2. Monthly rate (call it i). For a fixed rate: (1 + yearly rate ÷ 2)^(1/6) − 1. The Interest Act requires a mortgage to state its rate calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance1, and fixed rates are normally calculated half-yearly. For a variable rate: yearly rate ÷ 12. This site assumes monthly calculation for variable rates, which is common, but check your mortgage agreement.
  3. Number of payments (n) = years × 12
  4. Monthly payment = mortgage × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^(−n))
  5. Each month: interest = balance × i. Principal = payment − interest. New balance = balance − principal. Repeat.
  6. Equity = what the home is worth − what you still owe

The whole formula in one line

Fixed rate: Payment = P × [(1 + r÷2)^(1÷6) − 1] ÷ [1 − (1 + r÷2)^(−2 × y)] Variable rate: Payment = P × (r÷12) ÷ [1 − (1 + r÷12)^(−12 × y)]

P = mortgage amount (including any insurance premium) · r = yearly rate as a decimal (4.84% is 0.0484) · y = amortization in years
