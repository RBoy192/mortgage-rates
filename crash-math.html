Do the math yourself

  1. Mortgage amount = home price − down payment. If you put down less than 20%, add the insurance premium too.
  2. Rate per month (call it i). Fixed: (1 + annual rate ÷ 2)^(1/6) − 1, because Canadian fixed rates compound twice a year (opens in a new tab). Variable: annual rate ÷ 12, since variable rates usually compound monthly.
  3. Number of payments (n) = years × 12
  4. Monthly payment = mortgage × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^(−n))
  5. Each month: interest = balance × i. Principal = payment − interest. New balance = balance − principal. Then repeat.
  6. Equity = what the home is worth − what you still owe

The whole formula in one line (steps 2 to 4 combined):

Fixed rate: Payment = P × [(1 + r÷2)^(1÷6) − 1] ÷ [1 − (1 + r÷2)^(−2 × y)] Variable rate: Payment = P × (r÷12) ÷ [1 − (1 + r÷12)^(−12 × y)]

P = mortgage amount (plus any insurance premium) · r = yearly rate as a decimal (4.84% is 0.0484) · y = amortization in years
