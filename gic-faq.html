Rates Explained: GICs

FAQ

Short answers to the questions people ask most about GICs.

Tap a question to open it.

How much do I need to buy a GIC?

Often $500 or $1,000, depending on the lender and the GIC. For example, one bank asks for at least $500 for all its GICs1. Some special offers ask for more.

Can I lose money in a GIC?

The amount you put in is guaranteed, so it won't go down3. Deposit insurance also protects eligible GICs if the bank or credit union fails (see Is my money safe?). What you can lose is the chance to earn more elsewhere if rates go up after you lock in.

Can I take my money out of a non-redeemable GIC early?

Usually not: a non-redeemable (non-cashable) GIC locks in your money for the whole term2. If you might need it sooner, a redeemable (cashable) GIC or a savings account is a better fit. See Redeemable vs non-redeemable.

What happens when my GIC matures?

You can take the money out or put it into a new GIC at the rates of the day. Some banks renew it automatically if you don't tell them otherwise, at their renewal rates4, so mark the date and compare rates before it comes up.

How often is the interest paid?

It depends on the GIC: yearly, more often, or all at the end2. Compound GICs add the interest on and pay it all at the end. See How the interest adds up.

Are GICs covered by deposit insurance?

Yes. At CDIC member banks, eligible GICs are covered up to $100,000 per category, including interest5. Credit unions are covered by their province. See Is my money safe?.

Do I pay tax on GIC interest?

In a regular account, yes: interest counts as income, and you report it every year even if you only get it at the end6. In a TFSA it's generally tax-free. See Tax and TFSAs.

Can I hold a GIC in a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA?

Yes. Many banks offer their GICs in TFSAs, RRSPs, RRIFs, RESPs and FHSAs as well as regular accounts2. Rates can differ between them, so check.

What's a market-linked GIC?

A GIC whose return depends on how a stock market or a group of shares does. Your money is still protected, but the interest isn't known in advance3. This site only compares regular fixed-rate GICs.

Why is a lender's rate different from what this site shows?

This site shows each lender's advertised rate for a regular, non-registered GIC, and the special-offer rate when the lender lists one. Rates for TFSAs, RRSPs, big deposits or other GICs can be different. Always confirm with the lender.
