Rates Explained: Savings

What is a savings account?

A safe place for money you might need, that earns a little interest.

In brief
  • A savings account holds money you're not spending right away.
  • The bank pays you a little interest for keeping it there.
  • You can usually take your money out any time.

A savings account is a bank account for money you want to keep, not spend right away. The bank pays you interest for keeping it there.

Unlike a GIC, your money isn't locked in. You can usually put money in and take it out whenever you like.

Good for

  • An emergency fund.
  • Saving for something coming up soon.
  • Money you're not sure when you'll need.

Kinds of savings accounts

  • High-interest savings accounts pay more than basic savings accounts. These are the rates on this site's main page.
  • Some accounts are inside a TFSA, RRSP or FHSA, with their own tax rules (see Tax and TFSAs).

Savings accounts at CDIC member banks are covered by deposit insurance2 (see Is my money safe?).
