About these GIC rates

  • A GIC (guaranteed investment certificate) pays a fixed rate for a set term, such as 1 or 5 years. These are the lenders' advertised rates for non-redeemable GICs: your money is locked in until the term ends.
  • Rates are annual rates, with interest paid yearly or at the end of the term where the lender lists more than one option. Where a lender lists both a regular and a special-offer rate for a term, the special-offer rate is shown; some special offers are for new deposits only. Cashable and redeemable GICs usually pay less.
  • Many lenders need a minimum deposit, often $500 or $1,000, and some rates are for registered accounts (TFSA, RRSP) only. Check the details on the lender's page.
  • Deposits at banks are protected by CDIC (up to $100,000 per category), and at credit unions by their provincial deposit insurer. Check the coverage that applies to you.

Rates are collected from each lender's website every hour. Rates may be out of date or incomplete, so confirm the current rate and terms with the lender before you decide.
