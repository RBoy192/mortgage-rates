Rates Explained: Savings

Balances and tiers

Your rate can depend on how much you have saved.

In brief
  • Some accounts pay a different rate depending on your balance.
  • Each band of balances is called a tier.
  • Some pay the tier's rate on the whole balance, some only on the part in that tier.

Some savings accounts pay the same rate on every dollar. Others use tiers: the rate changes with your balance.

Two ways tiers work

Whole balance. Your whole balance earns the rate for the tier you're in.

Part in each tier. Each part of your balance earns its own tier's rate. Some banks work it this way1.

An example

Say an account pays 0.5% up to $10,000, and 1% above that. You have $15,000.

  • Whole balance: $15,000 × 1% = $150 a year.
  • Part in each tier: $10,000 × 0.5% + $5,000 × 1% = $50 + $50 = $100 a year.

Same rates, different result. So check how the tiers work before you compare.
