Lender scorecards

How the scorecard marks work

What each feature means, and the rule behind each mark.

Every value comes from the lender's own website or published documents, read by hand. For each lender the scorecard describes its main mortgage: the fixed-rate product whose rate this site shows, and its main variable-rate product for the variable-rate features. Values are checked again about every three months, and the date checked is shown with them.

Marks compare a feature with what's common among Canadian lenders. More flexible means it gives you more room or costs less if your plans change; more limited means less room or a higher cost. Features with no mark are a matter of preference. When a lender doesn't publish something, it's marked "Not published", never guessed, and it doesn't count for or against the lender.

FeatureRule
Lump-sum prepaymentsExtra you can pay each year without a charge, as a share of the original amount20% or more a year: more flexible. 15%: typical. 10% or less: more limited.
Payment increasesHow much you can raise your regular paymentUp to 100% (double): more flexible. 15% to 99%: typical. 10% or less: more limited.
Double-up paymentsPaying an extra amount, up to one more regular payment, on a payment dateOffered: more flexible. Not offered: more limited.
How the fixed-rate penalty is worked outFor a fixed rate, the charge is usually the greater of three months' interest or the interest rate differential (IRD). Working out the IRD from posted rates usually makes it larger.IRD based on the rate you actually pay: more flexible. IRD based on posted rates: more limited.

Assumable by a buyerA buyer taking over your mortgage, which can help when rates have gone upOffered: more flexible. Not offered: more limited.

Locking in to a fixed rateSwitching a variable rate to a fixed rate during the termAny fixed term, no charge: more flexible. No charge, but only to certain terms: typical. A charge applies or not offered: more limited.

What isn't scored yet

Some features matter but aren't published clearly by the big banks, so they aren't in the scorecard yet: whether the mortgage is registered as a standard or collateral charge, blend-and-extend options, and exactly which rate a lender uses to work out the interest rate differential. They'll be added for lenders that publish them.

Spotted something out of date? The lender's own terms always win: check your mortgage agreement or ask the lender. Information only, not financial advice.
