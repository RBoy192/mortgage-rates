Rates Explained: Savings

How the interest works

Interest is worked out every day and paid every month.

In brief
  • The rate is a yearly rate.
  • Interest is usually worked out every day on your balance.
  • It's usually paid into your account once a month.

Savings rates are shown as a yearly rate. A 2% rate on $10,000 earns about $200 over a year, if your balance stays the same.

Most banks work out your interest every day, on how much is in your account at the end of the day. Then they pay it into your account once a month1.

A quick example

$10,000 at 2% a year:

  • Each day: $10,000 × 0.02 ÷ 365 = about $0.55.
  • Each month: about $16.67.
  • Each year: about $200, a bit more once the monthly interest starts earning interest too.

Because interest is worked out every day, money you add starts earning right away, and money you take out stops earning right away.

Remember. Savings rates can change at any time, unlike a GIC's.
