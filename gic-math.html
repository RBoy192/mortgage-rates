Rates Explained: GICs

Do the math

Work out what a GIC will pay you, step by step.

In brief
  • Paid out: amount × rate × years.
  • Compound: amount × (1 + rate) to the power of the years.
  • Interest = what you get back − what you put in.

Interest paid out every year

Each year you get the same amount: interest each year = amount × rate

$5,000 at 3.5% for 2 years: $5,000 × 0.035 = $175 a year, so $350 in total.

Compound interest (paid at the end)

The interest is added on every year: what you get back = amount × (1 + rate)years

$5,000 at 3.5% for 2 years: $5,000 × 1.035 × 1.035 = $5,356.13, so $356.13 of interest.

Rates are yearly. For a rate like 3.5%, use 0.035 in the formula.
