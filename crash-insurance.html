Insured vs uninsured mortgages

If you put down less than 20% of the purchase price, you'll typically need mortgage loan insurance1, and the mortgage is called high-ratio. The insurance, also called mortgage default insurance, is provided by CMHC, Sagen or Canada Guaranty. It protects the lender if the loan isn't repaid, not you. In exchange for that protection, lenders can lend to buyers with smaller down payments, and CMHC says it lets lenders offer interest rates comparable to those generally reserved for borrowers with larger down payments2.

Insured (under 20% down)

  • You pay a one-time insurance premium that depends on the size of your down payment.
  • The premium can be added to your mortgage (and repaid with interest) or paid up front as a lump sum.
  • Some provinces charge provincial sales tax on the premium. That tax can't be added to your mortgage1, so it's paid separately.
  • The home must cost less than $1.5 million3.
  • Maximum amortization is 25 years4, or 30 years for first-time buyers and buyers of new builds (Department of Finance Canada3). A longer amortization adds a 0.20% surcharge to the premium.

Uninsured (20% or more down)

  • No insurance premium.
  • Also called a conventional mortgage.
  • Your lender sets the maximum amortization.
  • Required for homes of $1.5 million or more, which are above the insured price cap.

These match CMHC's premium table2, which lists them by loan-to-value ratio (a down payment of 5% to 9.99% is a loan-to-value of 90.01% to 95%, and so on). The same table shows a 0.20% surcharge for amortizations beyond 25 years, and a higher 4.50% premium in the top band when the down payment comes from a non-traditional source (CMHC's examples include loans and gifts). Example: 10% down on a $500,000 home means a $450,000 mortgage and a 3.10% premium of $13,950, so you'd borrow $463,950. The monthly payment calculator does this for you when your down payment is under 20%.

Minimum down payment

You need 5% of the first $500,000 of the price, plus 10% of the part between $500,000 and $1.5 million, and 20% at $1.5 million or more1. The $1.5 million cap on insured mortgages and these down payment rules took effect December 15, 20243.

Premiums and rules change. Confirm the details with your lender or mortgage professional.
