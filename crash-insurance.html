Mortgages Explained

Insured vs uninsured mortgages

What changes when your down payment is under 20%.

In brief
  • With less than 20% down, you need mortgage default insurance.
  • It usually costs 2.80% to 4.00% of the mortgage, and is normally added to what you borrow.
  • It protects the lender, not you, and is only available for homes under $1.5 million.

If your down payment is less than 20% of the price, you'll usually need mortgage default insurance1. A mortgage like this is called high-ratio. The insurance is provided by CMHC, Sagen or Canada Guaranty.

The insurance protects the lender, not you: it covers the lender if the loan isn't repaid. Because of that protection, lenders can lend to people with smaller down payments, and CMHC says it lets them offer rates similar to those for borrowers with larger down payments2.

Insured (under 20% down)

  • You pay a one-time insurance premium. The smaller your down payment, the higher it is.
  • The premium can be added to your mortgage (and repaid with interest) or paid up front.
  • Some provinces charge sales tax on the premium. That tax can't be added to the mortgage1, so you pay it separately.
  • The home must cost less than $1.5 million3.
  • The longest amortization is 25 years4, or 30 years for first-time buyers and buyers of newly built homes3. Choosing the longer amortization adds 0.20% to the premium.

Uninsured (20% or more down)

  • No insurance premium.
  • Also called a conventional mortgage.
  • Your lender sets the longest amortization.
  • Required for homes of $1.5 million or more.

What the premium costs

Mortgage insurance premiums

These rates come from CMHC's premium table2. An amortization longer than 25 years adds 0.20%. If the down payment comes from a non-traditional source (CMHC's examples include loans and gifts), the top rate is 4.50%.

Example. With 10% down on a $500,000 home, the mortgage is $450,000. The premium is 3.10%, or $13,950, so you'd borrow $463,950 in total. The monthly payment calculator adds the premium for you.

Minimum down payment

The least you can put down is1:

  • 5% of the first $500,000 of the price,
  • plus 10% of the part between $500,000 and $1.5 million,
  • or 20% if the price is $1.5 million or more.

These rules and the $1.5 million limit on insured mortgages took effect on December 15, 20243. Premiums and rules can change, so confirm the details with your lender.
