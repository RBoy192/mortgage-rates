Your rate may be lower. The rates on this page are the ones each lender advertises publicly. Many banks also offer relationship pricing (sometimes called relationship value, or RVR), which can give existing clients a rate lower than the posted rate, depending on the products they hold with the bank and their overall relationship. The rate you're actually offered also depends on your own situation, such as your down payment, credit and the type of mortgage. Ask each lender what relationship pricing they can offer before you decide.

    Mortgage crash course

    A mortgage in five pictures. The examples use the numbers from the payment estimator above, so change them there and watch everything here update.

    1What a mortgage is

    A mortgage is a loan to buy a home. You pay part of the price up front (the down payment) and borrow the rest. The home backs the loan: if you can't repay, the lender can sell it to recover what it's owed. Each payment covers interest, the lender's fee for lending, and principal, the amount you actually borrowed.

    2Term vs amortization

    Amortization is how long it would take to pay the mortgage off completely, commonly 25 years. Term is how long your current deal lasts, commonly 5 years. When a term ends you renew, and the lender offers a new rate for the next term. That's why one amortization usually means several terms.

    3Where your payment goes

    Your payment stays the same, but what's inside it changes. Interest is charged on what you still owe, so early payments are mostly interest. As the balance shrinks, more of each payment pays down principal.

    4What equity is

    Equity is the part of your home you own: what it's worth minus what you still owe. It starts as your down payment and grows as you pay the mortgage down. If the home's value rises, your equity grows faster, and if it falls, equity shrinks. These bars assume the value stays the same.

    5Do the math yourself

    1. Mortgage amount = home price − down payment
    2. Rate per month (call it i). Fixed: (1 + annual rate ÷ 2)^(1/6) − 1, because Canadian fixed rates compound twice a year. Variable: annual rate ÷ 12, since variable rates usually compound monthly.
    3. Number of payments (n) = years × 12
    4. Monthly payment = mortgage × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^(−n))
    5. Each month: interest = balance × i. Principal = payment − interest. New balance = balance − principal. Then repeat.
    6. Equity = what the home is worth − what you still owe
