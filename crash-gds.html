How much can you borrow? GDS and TDS

When you apply, a lender checks two ratios to decide how much you can borrow. Both compare your monthly costs to your gross monthly income, which is your income before tax.

  • GDS (gross debt service) counts housing costs only: your mortgage payment, property tax, heating, and half of any condo fees.
  • TDS (total debt service) counts those housing costs plus all your other monthly debt payments, such as car loans, credit cards, lines of credit and student loans.

For insured mortgages, CMHC's maximums are 39% for GDS and 44% for TDS (opens in a new tab), and the tighter of the two sets your limit. The mortgage payment is tested at a higher "stress-test" rate than you'd actually pay: the greater of your rate plus 2% or 5.25% (opens in a new tab). That way you're only approved for a payment you could still handle if rates rose.

GDS = (mortgage payment + property tax + heating + 50% of condo fees) ÷ gross monthly income TDS = (those housing costs + other monthly debt payments) ÷ gross monthly income Gross monthly income = yearly income before tax ÷ 12
