Mortgages Explained

How much can you borrow? GDS and TDS

The two tests lenders use to decide how much you can borrow.

In brief
  • Lenders check two ratios: GDS (housing costs) and TDS (housing costs plus other debts).
  • For insured mortgages the limits are 39% and 44% of your income before tax.
  • You're tested at a higher rate than you'll pay, so you'd still manage if rates rose.

Before lending, a lender checks how much of your income would go to housing and debt. It uses two ratios, both based on your gross income, meaning your income before tax.

  • GDS (gross debt service) counts housing costs only: your mortgage payment, property tax, heating, and half of any condo fees.
  • TDS (total debt service) counts those housing costs plus your other monthly debt payments, such as car loans, credit cards, lines of credit and student loans.

For insured mortgages, CMHC's limits are 39% for GDS and 44% for TDS1. Whichever limit you reach first decides how much you can borrow.

The stress test

Lenders work out your mortgage payment at a higher rate than you'll actually pay: your rate plus 2%, or 5.25%, whichever is higher1. This checks that you could still afford the payments if rates went up.

GDS = (mortgage payment + property tax + heating + 50% of condo fees) ÷ gross monthly income TDS = (those housing costs + other monthly debt payments) ÷ gross monthly income Gross monthly income = yearly income before tax ÷ 12
