Mortgages Explained

How much can you borrow? GDS and TDS

How the bank decides how much it will lend you.

In brief
  • The bank checks how much of your pay would go to your home and your debts.
  • For insured mortgages: home costs up to 39% of your pay, all debts up to 44%.
  • The bank tests you at a higher rate, just to be safe.

Imagine your monthly pay, before tax, is 100 coins. The bank checks how many of them would go to your home and your debts.

  • GDS counts the coins for your home: your mortgage payment, property tax, heating, and half of any condo fees. For insured mortgages, that can be at most 39 coins out of 100 (39%)1.
  • TDS counts the home coins plus the coins for your other debts, like car loans, credit cards and student loans. That can be at most 44 coins (44%)1.

If either number is too big, the bank lends you less.

The stress test

The bank also pretends your rate is higher than it really is: your rate plus 2%, or 5.25%, whichever is higher1. If you could still afford it, you pass. That way you'd be okay if rates went up.

The formulas, for the curious GDS = (mortgage payment + property tax + heating + 50% of condo fees) ÷ monthly pay before tax TDS = (those home costs + other monthly debt payments) ÷ monthly pay before tax
